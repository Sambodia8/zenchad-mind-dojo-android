# 10-Minute NSDR Protocol

- Meditation: nsdr
- Duration: 600 seconds
- Opening silence: 15 seconds
- Creative direction: A practical, breath-led non-sleep deep rest body scan with spacious pauses.
- Source basis: User-provided NSDR protocol transcript, cleaned for obvious transcription errors while preserving its instructions.

## 00:15

[softly] Welcome to this ten-minute non-sleep deep rest protocol. You can practise lying down or seated, though lying down may make it easier to rest. If you are not already comfortable, take a moment to settle your body. Close your eyes if that feels comfortable. Throughout the practice, breathe normally unless I invite you to do something different. There is nothing to achieve, and you do not need to force relaxation.

## 00:45

We will begin with three easy breaths. Inhale deeply, ideally through your nose, or through your mouth if that is more comfortable. Then exhale all of your air through thinly pursed lips, as if breathing through a small straw. Let us do that again. Inhale deeply... and exhale completely through gently pursed lips. One more time: inhale deeply... and exhale slowly until your lungs feel comfortably empty. Now return to your normal breathing. Allow the breath to be easy. If any breath feels strained, return to ordinary breathing.

## 01:35

In your mind's eye, imagine yourself standing over your body and looking down. Imagine holding a soft spotlight and directing it toward your feet. Focus on whatever your feet are in contact with: socks, sandals, shoes, the floor, or simply the air. Notice the sensations on the bottoms of your feet. There may be tingling, warmth, pressure, coolness, or very little sensation at all. Nothing needs to change. Simply notice.

## 02:15

Expand the spotlight to include the tops of your feet, your shins, and your calves. Then let it widen to include your thighs, your hamstrings, and everything down to your waist. With your lower body inside the light, inhale deeply through your nose or mouth. Exhale completely through gently pursed lips. Repeat that twice more, breathing comfortably. Each time you exhale, imagine the lower body sinking about a centimetre into whatever surface supports it. Then return to normal breathing.

## 03:00

Move the spotlight to your abdomen. Notice whatever your abdomen is sensing. As you inhale, your stomach may move out slightly. As you exhale, it may settle down. Let the spotlight expand to include your upper abdomen, your chest, and your neck. There is no need to control the breath now. Let it move in its own natural rhythm while you notice the sensations of the torso.

## 03:45

Now include both arms in the spotlight. Continue to breathe normally. Bring your attention to the places where your back is in contact with a shirt, a chair, a sofa, or the surface beneath you. Simply notice those points of contact. Inhale deeply through your nose or mouth. As you exhale, imagine your back and upper body sinking about a centimetre into the surface supporting you. Then allow the breath to become ordinary again.

## 04:35

Move the spotlight up to include your face, the top of your head, and the back of your head. Continue breathing normally. Focus on relaxing the muscles of your face: the jaw, the mouth, the cheeks, the eyes, and the forehead. See whether your exhales can become just slightly longer, without effort. Let the face be quiet and let the rest of the body be supported.

## 05:25

Now expand the spotlight downward to include your neck, chest, arms, abdomen, waist, legs, and feet. Imagine looking down at your whole body, illuminated inside this gentle spotlight. Deliberately dim the light, making the illumination less intense. Inhale deeply again through your nose or mouth. Exhale completely through gently pursed lips. As you exhale, imagine your entire body settling into the surface it is in contact with. Continue breathing normally.

## 06:20

Notice the whole body at once. You may be aware of many sensations, one sensation, or almost none. You do not need to chase any particular experience. Your attention can rest wherever it naturally rests. If the mind wanders, you can return to the feeling of contact between the body and the surface beneath you, or to the easy movement of the breath.

## 07:05

While continuing to breathe normally, move your toes ever so slightly. Notice that you can direct a small action and notice the sensation it creates. Move your ankles. Bend your knees just a little, only if that feels comfortable. You might move your upper body gently from side to side, or turn or nod your head very slightly. There is no need to make a large movement. Keep the rest of the body easy.

## 07:55

Now move your hands. Lift your arms a tiny amount and set them down again. Notice the contact and weight of the arms as they return to rest. You have been directing your attention toward sensations, and you have been choosing small actions while the rest of the body remains supported. Let your breathing stay comfortable and ordinary.

## 08:40

Take a final easy breath. As you exhale, let the entire body rest. Then begin to deepen the breath a little. Notice the room around you without rushing to respond to it. You may move your fingers and toes, stretch if you wish, and gradually bring movement back to the body.

## 09:20

[reassuringly] When you are ready, slowly open your eyes. Notice how visual information returns, while the sensations you have been attending to are still available. You can direct your attention toward different parts of the body, and you can choose how quickly to bring your actions and perceptions back into the room. Take your time returning. The practice is complete.
