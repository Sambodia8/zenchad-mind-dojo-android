# The Storm Inside the Snow Globe

- Meditation: urge-surfing
- Duration: 540 seconds
- Opening silence: 15 seconds
- Creative direction: An urge observed as a temporary storm moving through a snow globe
- Approval basis: Fourth variant for every narrated type authorized 2026-07-28

## 00:15

[softly] Settle somewhere safe and bring to mind an urge that is workable, not overwhelming. Imagine holding a snow globe without needing to shake or steady it.

## 01:00

Name the urge simply: wanting... checking... escaping... reaching. A name is a label on the glass, not an instruction.

## 01:50

Locate its strongest body sensation. Perhaps pressure, heat, restlessness, tightness, or a bright electric pull.

## 02:40

Imagine those sensations as snow moving inside the globe. Notice speed, density, direction, and where the storm is most active.

## 03:30

Let the breath remain natural. You are not blowing the snow away; you are staying present while gravity does its quiet work.

## 04:20

Rate the urge gently from zero to ten, then become curious about whether the number stays perfectly still.

## 05:10

Watch for a crest, a dip, or a change of texture. Even an urge that remains strong is changing in tiny ways.

## 06:00

[reassuringly] You can choose distance from the trigger, ask for support, or leave the situation. Surfing an urge does not mean refusing practical help.

## 06:50

Picture more flakes settling onto a miniature town. Windows reappear. Streets return. The storm was real, and it was also temporary weather.

## 07:40

Choose one next action that serves you better than the urge: wait five minutes, drink water, move rooms, message someone, or simply keep breathing.

## 08:30

[softly] Put the snow globe down in imagination. Feel your real hands and remember: a signal may be loud without becoming your commander.
