# The Observatory Closes for Dawn

- Meditation: nsdr
- Duration: 780 seconds
- Opening silence: 15 seconds
- Creative direction: An observatory powering down after a long night of watching stars
- Approval basis: Fourth variant for every narrated type authorized 2026-07-28

## 00:15

[softly] Lie down and let the surface beneath you take the full assignment of holding your weight. Imagine an observatory finishing its long night shift.

## 01:05

The great telescope stops turning. Feel the right foot, then the left... heels, arches, toes... each small instrument becoming still.

## 02:00

Let both calves and knees grow heavy. The metal stairs inside the observatory no longer need to carry anyone upward.

## 02:55

Soften the thighs and hips. A circular platform settles onto its resting locks with a quiet, dependable click.

## 03:50

Feel the lower back, middle back, and shoulder blades spreading across the support beneath you.

## 04:45

Let the belly move without supervision. The chest can rise and fall like the observatory dome breathing with the last dark hour.

## 05:40

Notice the right hand and arm... then the left. Fingers uncurl as control panels turn their small lights from green to blue.

## 06:35

Release the shoulders away from the ears. Let the throat remain open and uncomplicated.

## 07:30

Soften the jaw, lips, tongue, cheeks, eyelids, brow, and forehead. Even the astronomer of the mind may stop searching.

## 08:25

[reassuringly] Nothing needs to be solved during this interval. Thoughts can cross the dome like satellites and continue beyond the horizon.

## 09:20

Feel the whole body at once, a quiet building beneath an enormous sky. Rest is happening even if sleep does not.

## 10:20

Remain here while dawn gathers beyond the shutters. There is nowhere else your attention must report.

## 11:25

Begin to notice breath with a little more clarity. Invite movement into the fingers and toes, as softly as morning light entering.

## 12:25

[softly] Stretch if that feels welcome. Open your eyes when ready; the observatory can reopen slowly, one bright window at a time.
